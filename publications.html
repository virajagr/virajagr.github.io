  • Variants of Beam Search for DiskANN
    Viraj Agrawal, Kirankumar Shiragur.
    arXiv:2611.26609, November 2026. [pdf] [bibtex]
    Summary

    Graph-based data structures such as DiskANN, HNSW, and NSG constitute the state of the art for large-scale approximate nearest-neighbor (ANN) search. Indyk and Xu introduced the notion of α-reachable graphs, which include graphs built by DiskANN's slow-preprocessing construction, and proved that greedy search achieves an approximation factor of (α+1)/(α−1). Independently, Al-Jazzazi et al. proposed adaptive beam search (ABS) with a relaxation parameter γ > 0, showing a 2/γ approximation on navigable graphs (α ≈ 1), where greedy search admits no guarantees. In this work, we unify and generalize these results by proving that ABS with parameter γ on α-reachable graphs achieves an approximation of (α+1)/(α(γ+1)−1) for any metric. Moreover, on sorted α-reachable graphs (introduced by Gollapudi et al.) under the ℓ2 metric, we obtain the tighter bound max(α/(α(γ+1)−1), (α²+1)/(α²(γ+1)−1)). Beyond these theoretical results, we design a beam predictor using features inspired by fine-grained analysis of ABS, trained via a constrained objective that minimizes mean distance computations subject to a target recall. On three 100-million-point benchmarks, using graphs built by DiskANN's practical construction, beam predictor lowers mean query latency on SSD-resident indices at matched recall by up to 29.6% relative to classical beam search (CBS). Relative to ABS, it is faster in 11 of 15 settings, by up to 6.5%, and at most 1.4% slower in the other 4; at 95% recall, its advantage over ABS grows from 0.8–4.2% for 100 neighbors to 4.6–5.6% for 500 neighbors. In memory, it needs 1.3–23.7% fewer distance computations than CBS and 0.0–3.9% fewer than ABS.

  • Simple Linear Corrections for Compression in Late Interaction
    Mihir Agarwal, Sabyasachi Basu, Viraj Agrawal.
    arXiv:2610.14196, October 2026. [pdf] [bibtex]
    Summary

    Late-interaction (multi-vector) models store a vector per token, which makes their indices expensive, and the pooling and pruning methods used to compress them lose noticeable quality when compression is aggressive. We ask whether a lightweight change to the scoring function can recover that quality. For each encoder, compressor and vector budget we learn a single global linear correction by distilling the uncompressed model's score distributions over candidate documents; the encoder and compressor stay frozen, and the correction can be applied per query at search time or once, offline, to the stored vectors. Across ColBERTv2 and Jina v4 on MS MARCO and BEIR, and ColPali2 on ViDoRe v3, the in-domain correction improves over baseline compression in 154 of 160 BEIR settings, while transfer from MS MARCO is more variable.

  • Retrieval Needs Multivectors: An Exponential Separation
    Mihir Agarwal, Viraj Agrawal, Sabyasachi Basu, Ankit Garg, Kirankumar Shiragur.
    arXiv:2608.21494, August 2026. [pdf] [bibtex]
    Summary

    Multi-vector embeddings consistently outperform single-vector ones in retrieval, but the representational gap between them was poorly understood. Following Jayaram's work, we give the first explicit family of query and document sets, together with their relevance matrices, for which single-vector embeddings that rank all relevant documents above irrelevant ones require exponential size, whereas polynomial-size multi-vector embeddings suffice. This establishes an exponential separation in expressive power for the task of ranking documents. Motivated by the construction, we introduce ANDOR, a benchmark that naturally instantiates these hard examples: state-of-the-art single-vector models perform poorly zero-shot and improve only marginally with fine-tuning, while multi-vector models consistently do better and improve substantially with fine-tuning, closely matching our theoretical predictions.
